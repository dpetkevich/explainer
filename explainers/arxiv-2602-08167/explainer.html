How Robots Learn Which Thoughts Are Worth Thinking

Robot AIs can now write out their reasoning in plain text — 'the target is the red pepper; move the gripper left' — before moving an arm. But unlike a math problem, a robot's thoughts have no answer key. So today's systems force the robot to recite rigid templates. Those templates are mostly noise, and they add seconds of lag to every motion. This paper found a way for the robot to grade its own thoughts, using nothing but the probability it assigns to what a human expert actually did. The result: a robot that succeeds more often while thinking half as much.

Let the model grade its own thoughts

We ended Part 1 stuck: there is no answer key for a robot's reasoning. This part builds one out of the model's own probabilities — treat the right thought as a hidden variable, propose candidates in hindsight, and weigh each by how much it helps predict the expert's action.

Treat the right thought as a hidden variable between seeing and acting

Sample K thoughts, weigh them, keep one — and more samples provably get closer to the truth

With the weight defined, refinement is a weighted lottery: draw K hindsight thoughts and keep one with probability proportional to its weight. mathcalLK is a guaranteed lower estimate of logp(A|C), and it can only rise as K grows. Why now, and what it unlocks

The same self-grading loop, run unchanged on a robot arm, four legged robots, and a self-driving car, discovers a different reasoning diet for each body — something no hand-written template could. Because it needs zero external labels, it scales with nothing but logged demonstrations.

Different bodies discover different thoughts — with no labels, from logged demos alone

Everything so far ran on one arm and one pepper. Press the embodiment buttons: the same algorithm, unchanged, prescribes a different reasoning diet for each body. Over the no-reasoning baseline, the gains in task success are arm +28%, legs +101%, wheels +21% — no universal best template exists, which is why a self-grading filter had to replace hand-written ones.
